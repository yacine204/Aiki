In mathematics, an antiunitary transformation is a bijective antilinear map U: H 1 → H 2 U:H_₁→ H_₂ between two complex Hilbert spaces such that ⟨ U x, U y ⟩ = ⟨ x, y ⟩ ¯ langle Ux,Uyrangle =overline langle x,yrangle for all x and y in H 1 H_₁, where the horizontal bar represents the complex conjugate. If additionally one has H 1 = H 2 H_₁=H_₂ then U is called an antiunitary operator.
Antiunitary operators are important in quantum mechanics because they are used to represent certain symmetries, such as time reversal. Their fundamental importance in quantum physics is further demonstrated by Wigner's theorem. == Invariance transformations == In quantum mechanics, the invariance transformations of complex Hilbert space H leave the absolute value of scalar product invariant: | ⟨ T x, T y ⟩ | = | ⟨ x, y ⟩ | |langle Tx,Tyrangle |=|langle x,yrangle | for all x and y in H.
Due to Wigner's theorem these transformations can either be unitary or antiunitary. = == Geometric Interpretation == =
Congruences of the plane form two distinct classes. The first conserves the orientation and is generated by translations and rotations. The second does not conserve the orientation and is obtained from the first class by applying a reflection. On the complex plane these two classes correspond (up to translation) to unitaries and antiunitaries, respectively. == Properties == ⟨ U x, U y ⟩ = ⟨ x, y ⟩ ¯ = ⟨ y, x ⟩ langle Ux,Uyrangle =overline langle x,yrangle =langle y,xrangle holds for all elements x, y x,y of the Hilbert space and an antiunitary U. When U is antiunitary then U 2 U² is unitary. This follows from ⟨ U 2 x, U 2 y ⟩ = ⟨ U x, U y ⟩ ¯ = ⟨ x, y ⟩. langle U²x,U²yrangle =overline langle Ux,Uyrangle =langle x,yrangle. For unitary operator V the operator V K VK, where K is complex conjugation (with respect to some orthogonal basis), is antiunitary. The reverse is also true, for antiunitary U the operator U K UK is unitary.
For antiunitary U the definition of the adjoint operator U ∗ U^* is changed to compensate the complex conjugation, becoming ⟨ U x, y ⟩ = ⟨ x, U ∗ y ⟩ ¯. langle Ux,yrangle =overline langle x,U^*yrangle. The adjoint of an antiunitary U is also antiunitary and U ∗ = U ∗ U = 1. U^*=U^*U=1. (This is not to be confused with the definition of unitary operators, as the antiunitary operator U is not complex linear.) == Examples == The complex conjugation operator K z = z ¯, Kz=overline z, is an antiunitary operator on the complex plane.
